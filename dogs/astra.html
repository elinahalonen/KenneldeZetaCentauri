Astra is Grace's daughter, and the dog we kept from our first litter. If I am honest, she was not the obvious choice at seven weeks, and I spent a good part of her first year wondering whether I had kept the wrong one. She was quieter than her siblings and harder to read, more likely to work something out on her own than to come and tell me about it. What I mistook for an unremarkable puppy was really a particular kind of mind, and learning to read it properly is one of the things I am most grateful to her for.

Her way of meeting the world was there from the start, once I knew how to see it. There is a video of her as a small puppy going at a low chicken-wire fence, failing, sitting down to think, and trying again several times without ever getting upset. That is still exactly who she is. She meets a problem as though there is probably a way through it and she simply has to find it. In scent work she will locate the source, let me know she has it, and move on to the next thing before I have finished praising her. She works with me rather than for me, and she does not need me to tell her she got it right, because she already knows.

At work she is serious, quiet and completely absorbed. At home she is none of those things: affectionate to the point of pinning you down for a face wash, opinionated, funny, and able to switch off so thoroughly that she pours herself over the furniture like something that has given up on having a skeleton. Both of those dogs are entirely her. She has strong opinions and holds them loosely, and she has never once asked permission to be part of whatever is happening. She assumes she is included, and she is right.
