For Our Puppy Families

The First Year

The first year is not only for training the dog. It is for learning the dog well enough that training does not repeatedly put you at odds with who they are.

Planned coverage (draft notes — not final copy):

  • Build the relationship first; train the individual dog second.
  • Train recall, communication, household skills, safety, and the things that make freedom possible.
  • Avoid imposing so much formal structure that you stop being able to see the dog underneath it.
  • Learn motivational structure: intrinsic rewards, persistence, human recruitment, response to uncertainty, frustration, disengagement, social/environmental/food/search/object reinforcement.
  • Observe learning preferences and communication style.
  • Understand what arousal looks like before training scripts overlay it.
  • For breeder families, this also helps preserve information about inherited phenotype rather than only trained performance.
  • Use individual differences rather than generic puppy milestones as the only guide.

Source material to mine when writing: They're not puppies, they're small dogs, Reframing ADHD in dogs: a compassionate perspective, Embracing your imperfectly perfect dog.
