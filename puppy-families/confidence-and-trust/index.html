For Our Puppy Families

Confidence & Trust

For the dogs we raise, confidence should not be manufactured through pressure. The trusted human can become part of the dog's confidence system, and that relationship is too valuable to spend carelessly.

The goal is not to eliminate boundaries. It is to keep the relationship predictable and socially legible enough that the dog continues to trust the human as a safe base.

Planned coverage (draft notes — not final copy):

  • Do not try to build confidence by pushing through fear or hesitation.
  • Asymmetric risk: too much pressure can deepen fear and damage trust; too little pressure can usually be adjusted later.
  • Practical response: create distance, lower intensity, allow observation and voluntary approach, give time to process, increase challenge when coping well.
  • Confidence can be socially scaffolded: the dog borrows certainty from a trusted human.
  • The close PSM bond is double-edged: it powers cooperation but makes pressure from a trusted person potentially more consequential.
  • Micro-fissures: repeated incomprehensible friction and human frustration can make interaction slightly less safe/comprehensible without any dramatic rupture.
  • Dogs can perceive human emotional state without knowing the abstract reason behind the frustration.
  • Boundaries and a predictable “no” are not the same as conflict; volatility, ambiguity and rule-breaking inside the relationship are the problem.
  • The human should be socially legible, not endlessly permissive.

Source material to mine when writing: The priceless gift of resilience, Embracing your imperfectly perfect dog.
