Breeding Philosophy

The Basics

The normal stuff, plainly stated. If someone wants to know whether this is a careful, responsible breeding programme, they should be able to answer that without reading a philosophy essay.

Planned coverage (draft notes — not final copy):

  • Registration / pedigree framework and breed-club / regulatory context.
  • Health tests performed and why.
  • How breeding dogs are evaluated beyond health testing: functional structure, movement, temperament, working ability, family information.
  • Age and frequency of breeding; why litters are rare.
  • How a sire is chosen.
  • Pregnancy / veterinary care / practical preparation.
  • What puppies receive before leaving: veterinary care, microchip/registration, documentation, contract, puppy pack as applicable.
  • Return-to-breeder / rehoming expectations if relevant.
  • Lifetime support.
  • No grand claims — this page is the concrete baseline.

Source material to mine when writing: The work of a breeder, The work of a breeder: Part 2, What goes into the cost of a pedigree puppy?.
