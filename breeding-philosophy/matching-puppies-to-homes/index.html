Breeding Philosophy

Matching Puppies to Homes

The question is not which puppy is “best.” The question is what kind of puppy this is, and what kind of life will allow that individual dog to thrive rather than merely cope.

Planned coverage (draft notes — not final copy):

  • Understand family lifestyle, goals, experience, expectations and activities.
  • Understand puppy temperament, motivation, sensitivity, independence, working style and regulation.
  • Match dimensions rather than rank puppies.
  • Trait value is context-dependent: independent thinking can be an asset in search/tracking and a complication in precise handler-led work.
  • Use A-litter examples: Ambra for human-ID tracking; Wittra's liveliness paired with calm owners; Alba needed humour and comfort with a self-directed dog; Artis suited quiet but committed first-time owners.
  • Keeper decisions are also matching decisions: Astra was chosen partly as Grace's future life companion.
  • A family independently preferring the puppy they were matched to is useful anecdotal evidence, not “proof” of a method.
  • Placement is part of breeder responsibility, not a commercial transaction.

Source material to mine when writing: They're not puppies, they're small dogs, The work of a breeder, The work of a breeder: Part 2.
