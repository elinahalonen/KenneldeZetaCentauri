Breeding Philosophy

Doing Right by the Dog

The organising question is not “what will make us look successful?” but “what will this decision mean for the dog who has to live with it?”

I cannot make the world better for every dog. Breeding a very small number carefully is one concrete way I can try to make the future better for the dogs I bring into it.

Planned coverage (draft notes — not final copy):

  • Responsibility is first to the dogs being created, not breeder ego, titles or buyer convenience.
  • The dog's lived experience as the unit of evaluation.
  • Do not treat external validation as the purpose of the dog; use evaluation only when it genuinely informs decisions.
  • Physical soundness, mental capability, meaningful motivation, agency, security and recovery matter because they affect the dog's own life.
  • Joy and harmony with the family are legitimate outcomes, not sentimental extras.
  • Breeding as “stacking odds” rather than manufacturing perfection.
  • Human-dog relationships should be mutually enjoyable; a life with dogs should contain joy, not merely management.

Source material to mine when writing: Doing right by the dog, A love letter to my heart dog, Dog ethics, unpacked: Part 3, Embracing your imperfectly perfect dog.
