Breeding Philosophy

How We Raise Puppies

We do not think of puppies as a generic category to be processed through a checklist. They are small dogs, already beginning to show something about the adults they may become.

The breeder's job is to provide a safe, developmentally appropriate environment rich enough for bodies and minds to develop, while leaving enough space to observe the individual dog.

Planned coverage (draft notes — not final copy):

  • Before birth: dam welfare, prenatal environment, preparation.
  • First weeks: safety, maternal care, sleep, development appropriate to stage.
  • Prepared environment / “Montessori for puppies”: safe opportunities to explore, choose, manipulate, climb, balance, investigate and solve problems.
  • Physical development: varied surfaces, coordination, proprioception, functional movement, age-appropriate challenge.
  • Sensory and environmental novelty without turning exposure into a checklist or overstimulation.
  • Challenge and recovery: resilience matters because life will contain disruption; don't confuse resilience-building with pressure.
  • Rest and self-regulation as part of development.
  • Observe spontaneous behaviour: what interests each puppy, what frustrates them, how they solve problems, how they relate to people and siblings.
  • Use video as longitudinal evidence and to revisit what was actually visible at the time.
  • Do not over-train early to the point of obscuring the traits you are trying to understand.
  • Confidence-and-trust guidance belongs mainly after puppies leave, under For Our Puppy Families, rather than here.

Source material to mine when writing: The art and science of raising puppies: a behavioural scientist's perspective, Shaping sound minds and bodies: raising puppies with evidence-based enrichment protocols, Fireworks, puppies, and why early life matters more than we realise, The priceless gift of resilience, They're not puppies, they're small dogs.
